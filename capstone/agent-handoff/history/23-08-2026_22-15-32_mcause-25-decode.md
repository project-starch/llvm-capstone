# Reading an mcause 25 on this bitstream: three producers, and 7 ambiguous instructions

Written 2026-08-23 while helping the SQLite-wedge lane. Every step below was needed to turn
"the wedge shows 25" into a statement about what actually went wrong, and none of it was
written down anywhere. `capstone_unit.anvilh:288-300` already records that the *comments* were
off by one and cost three investigations; this note is about the two layers under that.

## 1. The debug bank packs a valid bit into the byte you read

`core/cva6.sv:1341-1342` (and the switch-255 mirror at `:1269-1271`):

```systemverilog
debug_led_o = {recent_nontrivial_trap_seen_log_q, recent_nontrivial_mcause_log_q[6:0]};
```

So a readout of **`0x99` is not mcause 153.** Bit 7 is the trap-seen valid bit and bits [6:0]
are the cause: `0x99` = seen + `0x19` = **25**.

**Only `mcause[6:0]` survives this bank.** Harmless for the capability causes (24-30), but a
cause >= 128 would alias silently, and nothing in the readout says so.

## 2. Two producers emit the number 25, and `tval` separates them for free

| producer | 25 means | writes to `tval` |
|---|---|---|
| FLU, `core/ex_stage.sv:488` (base 24, ordinal 1) | `UNEXPECTED_OPERAND` | rs1 **cursor** (`fu_data_i[0].operand_a`) |
| commit_stage, `core/commit_stage.sv:604` + `:226` (base **23**, ordinal 2) | `INVALID_CAPABILITY` — the PC capability's revnode went invalid (`!pc_revnode_valid_d`, `:224`) | the faulting **PC** (`commit_instr_i[0].pc`) |

Since `cva6.sv:1125-1136` latches `mcause`, `mepc` and `tval` in **one `if` block from one
`ex_commit` event**, and `mepc` is `pc_commit`:

> **`tval == mepc` -> commit_stage. `tval != mepc` -> FLU.**

This needs `tval` only to be *latched*, never *interpretable*, so it works on boots recorded
before anyone validated the tval path. Applied retroactively it excluded commit_stage outright across the **four** wedge boots
whose readout carried a tval (a fifth, boot 21, predates tval being in the readout) — no board
time.

## 3. mcause 25 does NOT universally mean "an operand lost its tag"

All 21 `UNEXPECTED_OPERAND` raise sites are guarded on a `NOT_CAP` test, but **seven are
disjunctions whose other arm is `!= NOT_CAP`** and fire on the opposite condition: an operand
that should have been a plain integer unexpectedly *being* a capability.

| NOT_CAP-only — 25 means a tag was lost | AMBIGUOUS — tag lost **or** unexpected capability |
|---|---|
| CINCOFFSETIMM, SEAL, SHRINKTO, CJALR, DROP, REVOKE, MREV, LCC, TIGHTEN, CALL, LDC, STC, DELIN | **CINCOFFSET (register form)**, SCC, INIT, SHRINK, CBNZ, SPLIT, RETURN |

`CINCOFFSETIMM` (`capstone_flu_unit.anvil:58`) is unambiguous; the register-form `CINCOFFSET`
at `:30` is `(rs1 == NOT_CAP) || (rs2 != NOT_CAP)` and is not. Same mnemonic family, opposite
conclusion.

**So a 25 constrains the fault only once the faulting instruction is known**, and `mepc` plus
`llvm-objdump` settles that offline. Do this before recording any "the capability lost its
tag" claim.

## What it yielded, the same evening

Applied to the SQLite wedge: `tval != mepc` in all five recorded boots excluded commit_stage,
then disassembly at `mepc` in the two surviving binaries showed the same instruction at both
sites, byte-identical (`5b 27 07 0b`):

```
104a50: ldc            a4, 0x0(a0)
104a54: cincoffsetimm  a4, a4, 0xb0
```

`CINCOFFSETIMM` is in the left column, so the 25 means the rs1 operand was `NOT_CAP` — and
structurally it could not have been otherwise: the immediate form has only one capability
operand, so there is no rs2 to be unexpectedly a capability, which is presumably why its guard
is a single test rather than a disjunction. Four boots direct; a fifth (boot 24) is an
inference from neighbours because its binary had been overwritten by a reused bake directory,
and is labelled as such rather than counted.

Net: a capability spilled by `stc` and reloaded by `ldc` 18 instructions later comes back with
`cap_type == NOT_CAP`, deterministically, across three binaries and six boots.

## Two incidental observations, neither verified against the spec

- **`SPLIT`'s guard is `&&`, not `||`** (`capstone_dyn_unit.anvil:117`):
  `(rs1_v == NOT_CAP) && (rs2_v != NOT_CAP)`. It does not raise when rs1 is NOT_CAP and rs2
  is also NOT_CAP — a hole where every sibling raises.
- **`SHRINK`'s guard is parenthesised in the S-11 shape** (`capstone_flu_unit.anvil:182`):
  `if(rd_in...==NOT_CAP ||rs1...!=NOT_CAP)||(rs2...!=NOT_CAP){`. S-11 is a live case in this
  tree of Anvil precedence silently collapsing such a condition, so the generated form should
  be read in the netlist before this line is trusted.

Both are NO SPEC BASIS CHECKED, recorded so they are not lost, not as findings.
