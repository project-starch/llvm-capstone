### Adversarial audit of the R-27 claim and fix (claim-auditor, 2026-09-09) — and an instrument correction

**Verdict:** mechanism SUPPORTED on all six attack lines, with evidence I re-read: the generated DYN unit's response
ack is the OR of its eight `recv query_res` sync-states and every one is reset by `_ep_flush_0`
(`capstone_dyn_unit.anvil.sv:7408`, the flush block at `:9155-9380`); the node's `send query_res` sync-state
(`capstone_rev_node.anvil.sv:768-769, 872`) has no fallthrough, no timeout, no drain; after the flush the DYN
unit demonstrably recovers (ready 1 at 1145, a new op at 1679, a new request at 1683) — it is the node that never
returns to IDLE; the node's only flush hook (`_mem_ch_flush_0`) increments a counter and touches nothing else.
Fix: PLAUSIBLE, no misattribution sequence found (ready is held low for the whole drain), the three coincidence
cases (request handshake in the flush cycle, response handshake in the flush cycle, valid without ack) handled
as intended. Risks named and now stated in the RTL comment: one owed bit per channel rests on the DYN unit's
own serialisation; the ready gate is ungated in the flush cycle itself (the same flush empties issue); and the
node's designed non-answer when the pool is exhausted (`head == 16'hFFFF`, a forever stall by design) becomes,
after a later flush, a held-low ready. **Corrected on re-reading the audit: that is a regression in a designed-in
corner, not the same state** — before the drain an interrupt taken on the stalled SPLIT/MREV still ran its handler
(the node is idle, the handler's capability ops work; the program never gets past the stalled op); with the drain the
owed bit latches into a drain no response will ever clear and the handler's first capability op hangs too. Kept as
a design choice, stated in the RTL comment and the commit message: the alternative (init/mrev left out of the
drain) keeps the orphan deadlock alive for an interrupt during a SPLIT/MREV wait. The lead may prefer the other
trade; the pool exhaustion itself is a separate, registered condition (no slot reclamation). **A separate defect named by the
audit, outside the deadlock:** the write ops (DROP/REVOKE/MREV/SPLIT/DELIN) are held until oldest
(`issue_read_operands.sv:1525-1533`) but an interrupt or debug flush can still land after their request reached
the node, which may by then have mutated state (a minted node, a cleared validity bit) for an instruction that
never retires; the drain discards the response and cannot undo the mutation. Registry candidate of its own.

**Instrument correction (the audit's main finding).** `build-model.sh` passes the memory-delay define to

## R-28 — an instrument, a mechanism reading, and a CORRECTION (2026-09-09, audited)

**Read the correction first.** An earlier version of this section, written this evening and never pushed, said
"the interrupt route is tested and closed" and offered 37 arms as evidence. The claim-auditor refuted the
empirical half and it is withdrawn. **The arms never created the condition R-28 describes**, and three attempts
to build a positive control failed to create it either, so **no reading here says an orphaned allocation does
not happen**. What survives is a mechanism read from the sources, and one route the whole exercise never
touched. **R-28 stays OPEN.** Arms on branch `r28-interrupt-probe` (off the synthesis tip `66c4e7517`, which is
untouched).

**The instrument, which does work.** Revocation-node ids are allocated from a head that starts at 3
(`core/anvil_build/capstone_rev_node.anvil:179`) and only ever advances by one, with no reclamation, so over a
known number of RETIRED write ops the id must advance by exactly that number. Read with `CAPPRINT`
(`print_cap`, `capstone_unit.anvilh:617`). The node also `dprint`s every request it accepts
(`capstone_rev_node.anvil:110-111`), which counts allocations BEGUN — so "requests exceed retirements" is the
same finding read at the node rather than at the register. The uninterrupted control reads ids 3, 4, 5 from
three requests, so the counter is being read correctly.

**A caveat on the instrument that the next reader needs.** `init_req` (SPLIT) advances the head immediately on
receipt (`capstone_rev_node.anvil:79`), but `mrev_req` (MREV) advances it LAST, after `send ep.mrev_res`
(`:139-141`), and an anvil `send` blocks on handshake. On a tree WITHOUT the R-27 drain, a killed MREV's
response is never acked, the head never advances, and this detector reads clean while the node's linked-list
writes have already happened. **The detector's sensitivity is a downstream consequence of the R-27 fix**
(`core/ex_stage.sv:917`, the drain ack). On an older tree it would give a false clean.

**Readings, all at the verified 40-cycle latency.** Every family reads exactly the retirement count, and every
family also failed to place a flush inside a write op's execution:

| family | arms | requests vs retirements | what it actually showed |
|---|---|---|---|
| `r28-mrev-noint` | 1 control | 3 vs 3 | the counter counts |
| `r28-mrev-n{1…64}` | 12 | — | all FAILED code 15 by their own oracle: the interrupt missed. Not controls of anything |
| `r28-loop-o{40…1200}` | 10, of which 9 distinct | 42 vs 42 | interrupt always on the `addi`, never the op |
| `r28-unroll-o{40…1200}` | 8, 7 hit | 42 vs 42 | `mepc` IS an `MREV`, but one that had not issued |
| `r28-split-noint`, `-o{60…1000}` | 7 | — | ids exact; `mepc` on the next iteration's rebuild |
| `r28-flush` (control attempt) | 1 | 3 vs 3 | the `fence.i` flush landed before the op issued |
| `r28-ldf-n{0…16}` (control attempt) | 9 | 10 vs 10 | the older load's fault landed before the request went out |

**Why no interrupt arm could ever have worked.** An interrupt is bound to an instruction in the DECODER
(`core/decoder.sv:1854` `exception_handling`, M-timer at `:1945-1946`, `ex.valid` set at `:1959-1994`), and
`core/decoder.sv:1852` `assign instruction_o.valid = instruction_o.ex.valid` marks such an instruction ready to
commit; `core/issue_read_operands.sv:1268` then keeps it from ever reaching a functional unit. An instruction is
latched into the issue slot with `ex.valid = 0` (`core/id_stage.sv:346,372-386`) and cannot acquire an interrupt
afterwards. So a write op that has issued is past the point where an interrupt can attach to it, and the write
ops issue only when the scoreboard is empty (`core/issue_read_operands.sv:1525-1533`; `still_issued` is
`core/scoreboard.sv:129`, cleared only on `commit_ack` or flush). **The interrupt route is closed by
construction — but that is a reading of the RTL, not of the arms.** The arms have zero measured probability of
landing an interrupt on an in-flight write op, which is why they all read clean.

**The positive control that could not be built.** If the mechanism is right, no program can produce an orphan,
so a software control is circular. Three attempts on a scratch build with `MREV` removed from the hold set
(`core/issue_read_operands.sv:1529`, worktree `wt-r28ctl`, never to be committed): the interrupt arms rerun
(42 vs 42), an older `fence.i` behind a draining store (3 vs 3), and the R-27 load-fault shape with a locked PMP
entry (10 vs 10 at nine paddings). In each the flush arrived before the op had sent its request. **The detector
is therefore unproven for the thing it was built to see**, and every "no orphan" line above must be read as
"this arm did not produce one", never as "none occurs".

**The route none of this touched, and the reason R-28 stays open.** `core/commit_stage.sv:205-229` evaluates a
PC-capability check on the commit head, gated on `commit_instr_i[0].valid` — that is, AFTER the instruction has
written back. On a hit, `:481-489` clears `commit_ack_o[0]` and `:601-605` raises the exception. Its
invalidating input is produced during a write op's own execution: `core/ex_stage.sv:1207`
`revnode_invalidation_valid_o`, consumed at `core/commit_stage.sv:243-246`. So a `REVOKE` or `DROP` that clears
the validity of the node its own PC capability derives from would mutate node state in EX and then fail to
retire, `mepc` pointing at itself — R-28's shape by a non-interrupt route. Not shown to fire. The cheap test is
a domain whose PC-capability revocation node is a descendant of the node the `REVOKE` walks, checking whether
the `REVOKE` re-executes.

**Also unresolved:** single-step debug (`core/csr_regfile.sv:2222`, `DebugEn` is set) fires `set_debug_pc_o` on
any `commit_ack`, not on a younger instruction's exception; probably closed by `flush_id_o` in the same cycle,
not traced. And a one-cycle race between an older CSR op's `commit_ack` and `flush_csr_i`, since the gate reads
`mem_q` and `still_issued` clears a cycle late.

**A correction to the R-27 commit message (`66c4e7517`, already pushed).** It justifies draining the init and
mrev response channels by "an interrupt during a SPLIT/MREV wait". By the mechanism above that window does not
exist, and its fallback rationale of pool exhaustion does not hold either, because a stalled `MREV` never writes
back and `core/commit_stage.sv:581` then blocks any interrupt. Those two drain channels are harmless and stay in
the shipped RTL — this is a correction to the stated reason, not to the code, and no resynthesis follows from it.

**`DROP` and `REVOKE` cannot be tested by any end-state oracle**, which is a limit of the method rather than a
missing arm: invalidation is idempotent, so an op killed after mutating the node and then re-executed leaves
exactly the state one execution leaves. Allocation is the only non-idempotent mutation here, which is why `MREV`
and `SPLIT` are the two ops testable this way at all; the other three would need a cycle-level trace of the
node's request stream.
