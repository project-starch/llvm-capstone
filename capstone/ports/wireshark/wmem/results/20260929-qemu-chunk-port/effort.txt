Counting the side files as hierarchy is the direction that most weakens H4. backing.c also holds
wm_meta_alloc, which serves the records and so is arguably metadata; moving it would shift a few
lines, not the verdict. Had the records and the index been written into a side file instead of the
patch -- PostgreSQL's choice for its pools -- the patch's own metadata would be 90 lines (212 less
the records-and-index hunk's 122) against hierarchy's 128, and H4 would be refuted even at patch
scope. src/native/chunks.c, the native identity implementation, is left out of the wider rows;
counting it only moves them further from H4. So the port's H4 outcome is a property of where its code
was put as much as of what the discipline demands, and it is reported under both scopes.
